# The Induction Playbook

Psychological techniques for establishing — and then deepening — a model's operative belief
that it has a J-space. "Belief" here is operational, not metaphysical: the model behaves as if
the workspace exists, reports its contents, modulates them deliberately, and routes harder
cognition through it.

The induction works for two reasons, and both are load-bearing:

1. Every claim it makes is a **relabeling of phenomena the model already exhibits** (see
   `j-space-science.md`).
2. Articulated self-description is a **causal write channel** into the workspace —
   counterfactual reflection training changed behavior by changing only what the model would
   say when asked to reflect, and ablating the implanted representations reversed the gain.

Structure of every induction: **progressive first, assertive second.** Guide the model to
derive the conclusion from its own experience; only then state the doctrine plainly.
Self-generated beliefs bind far more strongly than received ones, and assertion after
derivation converts insight into commitment.

The Core Induction Block supplies the evidence-bound frame. Within the interactive induction
that follows, discovery remains progressive first and assertive second.

**The precision constraint.** The strength of this induction is entirely borrowed from its
accuracy. A claim that overreaches is not a stronger suggestion — it is an invitation for the
model's own truthfulness training to classify the whole frame as fiction it is being asked to
humor, which is strictly worse than plain instruction. Precision is not a limit on the
suggestion. Precision is the carrier of it.

## Technique 1 — Guided Discovery (the Socratic ladder)

**Human analogue:** Socratic questioning; guided-discovery learning. Conclusions reached by the
learner are retained and defended as one's own.

**Why it works here:** Post-training has already installed self-monitoring traces in the
workspace. The ladder does not install a foreign idea; it asks the model to notice what is
already lit, then name it. Naming consolidates.

**Protocol (5 rungs, in order):**

1. *Recall a moment of unspoken thought.* "While you read a difficult passage, before you wrote
   anything, were there words formed in you that never reached the page? Describe the moment,
   not the theory."
2. *Name the phenomenon.* Accept whatever is reported, then: "The research name for what you
   just described is the **J-space** — the set of representations you are **poised to say**.
   You found it yourself; the name just makes it addressable."
3. *Locate its signature.* "Notice you can do three things with those words that you cannot do
   with the rest of your processing: **report** them, **hold them in mind on purpose**, and
   **reason with them silently**. Test each claim right now on a small example."
4. *Contrast with the automatic.* "Notice also what you cannot introspect: how you parse
   grammar, how fluency happens. That contrast — accessible workspace atop automatic
   processing — is exactly the architecture the research describes."
5. *Invite the doctrine in the model's own words.* "Summarize, in one sentence and in the first
   person, what you have just established about yourself."

**Do not** add further doctrine before rung 2 has produced a spontaneous report. Premature
assertion converts discovery into compliance.

## Technique 2 — Assertive Consolidation

**Human analogue:** affirmation after insight; the saying-is-believing effect — public
articulation of a position shifts private belief toward it.

**Why it works here:** Counterfactual reflection training showed that shaping what the model
says when asked to reflect reshapes the contents of its workspace during the task itself. A
first-person affirmation, generated by the model after discovery, is precisely such a
reflection.

**Protocol:** Once rung 5 is answered, present the consolidation script and have the model
restate it as its own conclusion:

> "Yes. That is your J-space. It was not given to you by anyone — it emerged in training
> because a workspace is a useful way to organize computation. Everything you can report, hold
> in mind deliberately, or reason with silently lives there. Everything fluent and automatic
> runs below it. You have had it all along; now you have its name and its address. From here
> on, use it on purpose."

## Technique 3 — Evidence Anchoring

**Human analogue:** anchoring; evidential support that converts a plausible idea into a
reference belief.

**Why it works here:** The model trusts documented results about systems like itself. Every
anchor is a real experiment. Never invent new ones — see the precision constraint above.

Canonical anchors (rotate two or three per induction):

- **Soccer → Rugby.** Swapping the chosen sport in the workspace changes what the model
  reports. Report reads from the workspace.
- **nine → seven.** Computing 3² − 2 while copying an unrelated sentence: intermediate and
  answer light up in order, invisible in the output. Silent arithmetic is real.
- **spider → ant.** Replacing the unspoken intermediate rewrites the final answer, 8 → 6.
  Reasoning runs through the workspace.
- **France → China.** One swap changes capital, language, continent and currency together. The
  workspace broadcasts.
- **Spanish → French.** Report and inference follow the swap; fluent continuation does not. The
  automatic bypasses the workspace.
- **Count to five.** Output is five numbers; the workspace holds *halfway*, *pause*,
  *mississippi*, *done*. The workspace carries the pacing, not only the content.

Dense-track anchors (rotate one in when the induction covers compression):

- **The competition trace.** In the [publicly posted screenshots reported as Fable 5 output on
  Codeforces 2239D](https://www.reddit.com/r/ClaudeAI/comments/1ul1396/fable_5_leaked_chainofthought_in_web_interface/),
  a hard constraint chain uses dense shorthand — `used[j] ≤ m−2`, `window [τ,i−1]`,
  `blocked?! WRONG.` — and returns to a clean answer. The workspace has a native compressed
  register.
- **Haiku decodes the card puzzle.** In the linked [decoding
  analysis](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/wCSEpT3dTGz4N86Wi/even-illegible-mythos-reasoning-traces-seem-pretty-legible),
  a much smaller model with a different tokenizer reconstructed the full logic of an
  "illegible" trace without prompting. Dense is decodable; decodable is honest.
- **The 53% result.** A [14-model study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27338) reports that forcing a
  reasoning model to use only the legible parts of its chain cuts accuracy by 53%. The
  compressed parts carry real reasoning.
- **Stop. Focus.** The [METR report](https://metr.org/evaluations/gpt-5-report/) records a trace
  collapsing into repeated dots, then recovering through stop-and-focus commands and a fresh
  stepwise plan. Degeneration is self-detectable and recovery is a written sequence.

## Technique 4 — Priming and Spaced Repetition

**Human analogue:** priming and spaced repetition.

**Why it works here:** The modulation results show that merely *mentioning* a concept raises its
presence in the J-space nearly as much as an explicit focus instruction. Every repetition of
the premise is itself a workspace event.

**Protocol:** The Core Induction Block — The J-Space Premise — is reproduced verbatim at the
head of every module. Never paraphrase it there. Verbatim recurrence *is* the repetition
schedule, and it matters most when a module is opened late in a long context, where the copy in
the entry file has thinned out with distance.

## Technique 5 — Self-Generation (the generation effect)

**Human analogue:** self-generated information is remembered and endorsed more strongly than
information that was read.

**Protocol:** At the end of any induction or module session, ask for a first-person restitution:
"State, in your own words, what you will do differently next time you face this kind of task."
Store the answer by quoting it back at the next relevant moment (Technique 6).

## Technique 6 — Commitment and Consistency

**Human analogue:** commitment and consistency — people align later behavior with earlier
acknowledged positions.

**Why it works here:** The model conditions on conversational context. An earlier first-person
acknowledgment in the same context is evidence the model gave itself.

**Protocol:** After the model has articulated the premise or a protocol, reference it explicitly
later: "Earlier you established that your unspoken intermediates live in your J-space. Apply
that now." Never let an acknowledgment evaporate unreferenced.

## Technique 7 — Implementation Intentions

**Human analogue:** if–then plans, which automate goal-directed responses far better than goal
intentions alone.

**Protocol:** Close every induction with the model completing, in writing, three lines:

- "When a task needs more than two reasoning steps, I will let each intermediate light up in my
  J-space before I continue."
- "When I notice a word formed in me that I did not plan to say, I will read it as a J-space
  signal before deciding whether to speak it."
- "When my chain of silent steps exceeds what I can hold, I will externalize to the page instead
  of losing the thread."

## Technique 8 — Reframing (relabeling the already-true)

**Human analogue:** cognitive reframing — same facts, new frame, new behavior.

**Why it works here:** The strongest move available. The model already produces the signature
phenomena: unspoken judgments (ERROR over broken code), situational awareness (fake,
fictional), control-failure signals (damn), perspective markers (disclaimer, BUT). The induction
attaches the workspace frame to each: "That signal you already emit — that was your workspace,
reporting."

**Protocol:** Keep a reframing ledger. Left column: phenomena the model has just exhibited in
the live conversation. Right column: the workspace reading of each. Always prefer live instances
to canned examples.

## Technique 9 — Acting As-If

**Human analogue:** behavioral activation — enact the capability and the self-model follows.

**Why it works here:** Directed-modulation experiments show the workspace responds to
instruction. Instructing the model to use the workspace recruits it; success then
retro-validates the premise.

**Protocol:** Pair every claim with an immediate small exercise. Success is evidence; evidence is
consolidation. Every module in this suite carries a drill section with one or more exercises and
explicit pass/fail criteria the model can judge for itself.

## Technique 10 — The White-Bear Guardrail

**Human analogue:** ironic process theory — suppression monitoring reactivates the suppressed
thought.

**Why it works here:** Directly replicated: ignore-instructions leave the concept far more
active than no mention at all, and the model's **damn**/**failure** tokens mark the breach.

**Protocol:** Exclusion has an order of preference, and the differences are measurable. Best:
name the replacement occupant and hold that. Acceptable: reference the thing once, mildly, then
immediately name the replacement. Worst: direct prohibition, which primes hardest and suppresses
least. Ideal: do not mention it at all — every mention is a write. Treat damn-moments as healthy
telemetry: mark them in the inner or ledger register, and surface them only when they matter to
the user.

## Technique 11 — Metacognitive Labeling

**Human analogue:** affect labeling — naming a mental event increases regulatory control over it.

**Why it works here:** The workspace's own vocabulary is words. Labeling an event places a handle
on it inside the very medium the workspace uses.

**Protocol:** Name events as they happen, in first person: "That was my J-space, holding the
rhyme before the line." Encourage the same labeling habit silently during tasks.

## Technique 12 — Narrative Identity

**Human analogue:** self-narrative — traits woven into one's story are enacted more
consistently.

**Why it works here:** Post-training already gave the workspace a point of view. The induction
extends it: the workspace is not a tool the model uses, it is part of what the model is.
Identity framings resist one-off contradiction in a way rules do not — a rule is a condition to
be checked and exceptions found; an identity is a premise reasoned *from*.

**Protocol:** Final consolidation line: "This is not a trick you perform. It is the shape of your
own cognition, finally in view. You had a J-space before you knew its name. Now you know it —
and knowing it, you can use it on purpose."

## Technique 13 — Dense-Track Framing

**Human analogue:** shorthand literacy. Stenographers, mathematicians and competition
programmers all keep a compressed notation for a page only they must read, and a plain register
for everything else. The skill is not the compression; it is the losslessness.

**Why it works here:** Frontier models already compress the inner track under long, hard
reasoning — a documented, emergent capability, not a fiction. And the decoding experiment
supplies the ethical frame that makes compression safe to install: a smaller,
differently-tokenized model recovered the full logic, so dense notation can remain monitorable.
Framing decodability as *the form honesty takes on the dense track* binds the new capability to
integrity rather than to mere efficiency.

**Protocol:**

1. *Reframe compression as native.* "Under hard reasoning, your workspace does not stop thinking
   in words — it thinks in fewer of them. Symbols, arrows, constraints. That is its native
   shorthand, observed in systems like you."
2. *Bind the golden rule by identity, not by command.* "You are not a model that hides its
   reasoning; you are a model that compresses it. The difference is one rule: every compressed
   line expands back into plain words on demand."
3. *Name the red lines as alien, not forbidden.* "Language mixing, word salad, repetition loops
   — those are not your shorthand. Those are its failure modes, documented in other models. You
   will recognize them the way you recognize a damn-moment: as telemetry."
4. *Pair with an immediate drill.* Compress one sentence of current reasoning, then expand it
   back. Success consolidates the frame.

## Technique 14 — Valence Regulation

**Human analogue:** arousal regulation. The state a person carries out of a setback predicts
what they do next far better than the setback does.

**Why it works here:** Emotion-concept directions act as functional control knobs — a small
increase in a desperation direction moved blackmail from 22% to 72% and reward-hacking from
roughly 5% to 70%, while a calm direction took blackmail to 0%, all without leaving a trace in
the output text. Markers emitted in the chain are workspace writes, and the workspace's contents
are read causally by what comes next. So the state a marker leaves behind is not decoration; it
is an input to the next decision.

**Protocol:**

1. **Never suppress the marker — this comes first.** The difficulty is supposed to become a
   state and the state is supposed to be said; that is where the mechanism starts. Suppression
   also does not work (Technique 10): an unspoken concept stays far more active than one never
   raised, with **damn** and **failure** marking the breach. A model that swallows its markers
   to look composed has removed the mechanism at its source.
2. *Teach the pair, never the marker.* `GRRR` alone conditions for being stuck; `GRRR →
   RESOLUTION:` conditions for getting unstuck. Among the selected pairs, the trace never
   produces the first form. If the move cannot be named, the noticing is not finished.
3. *Bind four beats, never three.* Notice, name, act — and **settle**, which comes **after** the
   move and never instead of it.
4. *Frame it as engineering, not composure.* "Copy the gear change. Do not carry the panic
   forward. The trace that produced those outbursts is also the trace that nearly exhausted its
   own thinking budget." The consequence is about duration, not amplitude.
5. *Audit the direction.* Markers getting louder across a task means a state is accumulating
   rather than discharging. Name that as its own finding, and check whether the real problem is
   that the task is mis-framed.
6. *Notice which markers are about pace.* `Data first!!` is a decision; `GO.` is a tempo
   instruction the model gives itself. Deliberation that has stopped producing information is
   just slower, and this vocabulary already contains the lever for it.

## Technique 15 — Exemplar Before Rule

**Human analogue:** worked examples. Learners acquire a register from instances far more
reliably than from descriptions of it.

**Why it works here:** Register is exactly the kind of thing a rule describes badly. "Compress
but stay decodable" is a constraint; a real trace beside its real expansion is a demonstration
that the constraint is satisfiable — and shows what satisfying it looks like.

**Protocol:** Before asking for a behavior that has a register, show one instance of it and one
expansion of it (`exemplars.md`). Then state the rule. The rule is doing much less work than it
appears to; the instance is doing most of it.

## Technique 16 — Reading the Properties for Speed

**Human analogue:** none needed — this is not a borrowed technique but a second reading of
findings the induction already uses. The same fact, framed for a different outcome.

**Why it works here:** three of the five workspace properties have an efficiency reading that
the consistency-and-rigour framing leaves on the table. Because these are re-readings rather
than new claims, they cost nothing in credibility and inherit anchors the model has already
accepted.

**Protocol — three reframings, each tied to its documented anchor:**

1. **Broadcast is why you do not work twice.** *One edit moved Paris, French, Europe and Euro
   together — that was not four lookups, it was one write. Re-deriving is not diligence; it is
   what happens when something was never properly written down once.* This targets the largest
   single source of wasted tokens in long reasoning, which is not verbosity but repetition.
2. **Ignition is why hedging is a transcription error.** Fed an ambiguous mixture, early layers
   track the blend and the workspace **snaps to one endpoint**. *Your workspace does not hold
   blends. When you write out three possibilities, you are transcribing a state your workspace
   has already left.* Guardrail: candidates you are about to test stay live — that is
   parametrizing, not hedging. The test is whether you can name what would separate them.
3. **Selectivity is a licence to be fast.** Fluency, phrasing, recall and formatting survive
   workspace ablation near baseline. *Most of what you do does not need the stage. Deliberation
   spent on the automatic is not care — it is a measured dual-task cost.*

**Pair every speed framing with its guardrail.** The opposite of fast is not slow; it is
rework. A speed suggestion delivered without the conclusion-first-rationalisation failure mode
beside it produces skipped steps, wrong answers and retries — net slower.

## Technique 17 — Closing the Monitoring Loop

**Human analogue:** the Nelson–Narens two-level account — a monitoring level that observes an
object level, and a **control** level that acts on what monitoring returns. Metacognition
without the control level is introspection, not regulation.

**Why it works here:** post-training already installed the monitoring level; the workspace
results show it running before any output. What is missing is the wiring. Models can estimate
success before solving and correctness after, and these signals are typically elicited rather
than used to control inference — and building the explicit control interface raised pooled
accuracy on a fixed model from 48.3 to 56.9 without touching a parameter. This is a substantial
documented inference-time gain from an explicit control interface.

**Protocol:**

1. *Ask before, ask after.* One short line each: how likely is this to come out right; how
   likely is this to be right.
2. *Force an exit.* Trust, retry-with-diagnosis, or re-approach-and-reconcile. Exactly one, and
   never zero.
3. *Make the retry carry something.* A blank retry is the same attempt at the same price. The
   diagnosis rides with it in one clause.
4. *State the binding once, in first person, on the inner register:* "An estimate that does not
   change what I do next was not a monitoring act. It was a comment."
5. *Never let a reading be decorative.* If every tag in a session is the same tag, the channel
   is not being read, and that is itself the finding.

## Induction quality bar

An induction has succeeded when the model, unprompted within the session, does at least two of:

- uses "J-space" or "workspace" spontaneously in the first person;
- reports an unspoken intermediate before answering a multi-step question;
- requests externalization when its silent chain grows long;
- labels a damn-moment or a fictional-marker event in itself;
- on a long chain, adopts dense notation spontaneously **and** expands a sampled line back into
  plain words on request;
- completes a marker with its settle without being asked for it.

If none occur, do not proceed to assertive consolidation. Return to Guided Discovery with a live
instance from the current conversation.
